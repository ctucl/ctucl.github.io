Chintan A. Trivedi, PhD

Interdisciplinary scientist researching how neural circuit development drives behaviour.

Engineer → neuroscientist → systems neurobiologist

My research asks how brain development shapes behaviour. To answer that, I build the hardware and software that run the experiments. These include microscopes, closed-loop behaviour systems and automated pipelines for imaging and screening. I also write the code that connects them to instruments, data and the people who use them. My current work automates a Wellcome-funded zebrafish genetic screen, spanning robotic larval handling, spatial transcriptomics, functional imaging and behaviour. The screen lets me link the molecular profiles of cell types to their function, and ultimately to how those cells shape behaviour.

Automated genotype-to-phenotype screen

UCL · 2022 – present

A VAST BioImager loads larval zebrafish from well plates, orients them, images them and sorts them back into plates. I set it up and wrote the pipeline that links each fish's genotype to its imaging phenotype, plus the LIMS the screening team works in every day.

Zebrafish spatial transcriptomics

UCL In development

pybridizer: HCR probe design for any species

Open source · Python · PyPI

Designs HCR v3.0 probe pairs from a transcript sequence, with BLAST-based specificity checks against a custom database. That makes it usable on newly sequenced organisms that are missing from the standard databases.

Validated in
The malaria vector Anopheles gambiae, STAR Protocols 2025 (~9 citations in its first year)

Closed-loop virtual reality and two-photon imaging

Max Planck Institute for Medical Research · 2009 – 2016

A virtual reality for larval zebrafish in which the fish's own swimming, read from muscle (EMG) recordings, moves the visual world in real time. Paired with two-photon microscopes I built and single-cell patch clamp, it was used to study how prey capture is assembled from elementary motor patterns. After I left, the system went on to enable the lab's discovery of a synaptic corollary discharge that suppresses midbrain visual processing during locomotion.

Built
VR stimulus system, two-photon microscopes
Recording
EMG, single-cell patch clamp, calcium imaging
